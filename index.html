What you get, and how pricing works

Every deployment includes the same core: document Q&A with page citations, roles and access control, approval workflows, the audit chain, backups, updates, and the deployment itself. The core is never itemized.

Two choices decide the quote. The configuration decides where the system runs and what data it can carry. The service level decides how much of the operation we take on. The same service levels apply to Cloud and In-Office.

Essential care. Monitoring, updates, a response window, a quarterly review, and keeping the deployment running. For a business that wants the system watched while its team runs it.

Managed operations. Essential care, plus support hours, one new workflow a quarter, and a monthly review. The default for a business that does not want to run AI infrastructure.

Compliant operations. Managed operations, plus compliance artifacts, audit support, and the evidence pack. For regulated work, with the agreements in place.

Pricing has three parts: a one-time discovery, a one-time build, and a monthly service level. Discovery is a fixed fee, credited against the build. The build is quoted for the work: document ingestion, connectors, workflow builds, training, cutover, and acceptance testing. The monthly fee is the service level you choose.

Cloud clients pay their cloud provider directly for usage. We do not bill tokens, and we do not pass through cloud costs. In-Office clients buy the hardware at our cost plus procurement, then pay for power and maintenance. Where scope varies we quote it: extra seats, connectors, workflows, and document volume. Vertical editions are quoted with the build.

Which one fits

Cloud Standard works well if you have a non-regulated business, want current frontier models, and want to get up and running quickly while your cloud provider bills you for usage.

Cloud Compliant works well if you have a regulated or government business and need the agreements, controls, and evidence that go with regulated work. It runs on compliance-grade services in your own account.

In-Office works well if you want your data never to physically leave your building, or the system has to keep working without an internet connection. You own the hardware, and there are no per-token costs. You do pay for power and maintenance, and a service contract is optional. An entry system starts around $30,000 in hardware. A model in the same class as the cloud's frontier models starts around $400,000 and needs a server room.

If you're not sure, email us. We'll help you find the option that fits or answer any questions you might have.

What In-Office hardware costs

In-Office is the hardware product, so most of the cost is the model. A current open model at full precision, running on one workstation, starts around $30,000 in hardware. That model is good, but it is not a frontier model, and the answers show it.

A local model in the same class as the cloud's frontier models needs a server room and about $400,000 in hardware. The current top open-weight model, at full precision, is a data-center machine: six servers, 48 GPUs, and more than $2 million.

There are no per-token fees at any size. Power, cooling, backups, updates, and an optional support contract are real monthly costs. On an entry system, power runs a few hundred dollars a month. A support contract, if you want one, is quoted with the system.

That is why we offer both. Use the cloud when your documents are allowed to leave. Use In-Office when they are not, and budget for the hardware the job actually needs.

How it works

It's not magic, it's more like a layered cake. One layer reads your documents and indexes them. One layer decides which AI model answers what, and logs it. One layer is the chat you actually see. One layer does the grunt work: email drafts, approval routing, reminders. Cloud runs in your cloud account. Standard uses ordinary cloud services; Compliant uses compliance-grade services under signed agreements. In-Office runs the same system on your hardware, with open weight models and no internet dependency.

Start where you are. Move when the rules change.

You can start with either product. Cloud Standard is the most economical in the beginning. Within Cloud, moving to Compliant is a configuration change in the same account. Moving from Cloud to In-Office is a deployment change, not a rebuild, because both run the same system. That is the point of building one system and selling two ways to run it.
